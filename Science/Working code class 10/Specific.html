Guided Experiment

Replicating the textbook activity: understanding the relationship between mass (m), temperature change (ΔT), and heat energy (Q).

Specific Heat Capacity Concept

Definition: Specific heat capacity (c) is the amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1°C (or 1 K).

The Formula

Q = m × c × ΔT

Where:

  • Q = Heat energy (J)
  • m = Mass (kg)
  • c = Specific heat capacity (J/(kg·°C))
  • ΔT = Temperature change (°C) = (Final − Initial)

Summary & Notes

Key Takeaways

  • Water has a high specific heat capacity (≈ 4200 J/(kg·°C)), meaning it heats up slowly.
  • Metals usually have lower specific heat capacity, meaning they heat up quickly.
  • Darker objects absorb more radiation (heat) than lighter ones.
