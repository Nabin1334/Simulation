Hard Shadows

Hard shadows have sharp, clear edges with a distinct boundary between light and dark areas. They occur when sunlight comes directly from a small, bright source like the sun on a clear day.

When You See Hard Shadows

Look for hard shadows around noon on a sunny day. Your shadow will have very sharp edges and be directly beneath you or slightly to one side.

Soft Shadows

Soft shadows have blurry, gradual edges that fade from light to dark. They happen when clouds scatter sunlight, creating a larger, diffused light source.

When You See Soft Shadows

On cloudy or overcast days, shadows appear soft and fuzzy. The edges are less defined and the transition from shadow to light is gradual.

Contact Shadows

Contact shadows are the dark areas right where objects touch surfaces. They make objects look like they're firmly planted instead of floating.

When You See Contact Shadows

Look at your feet on a sunny day - you'll notice a darker area right under your shoes where they meet the ground. This is a contact shadow!

Shadow Science Explained

Light Travels in Straight Lines

Sunlight travels in straight lines from the sun to Earth. When these light rays hit an object like a person, they can't pass through, creating a shadow on the opposite side.

Shadow Direction

Shadows always point away from the light source. In the morning, shadows point west. At noon, they're shortest. In the evening, shadows point east.

Shadow Size Changes

When the sun is low in the sky (morning/evening), shadows are long. When the sun is high (noon), shadows are short. Try moving the sun slider to see this!

Weather Effects

On cloudy days, shadows are softer because clouds scatter sunlight in many directions. On clear days, shadows have sharp, well-defined edges.
