Sound Propagation in Different Media

Solids (Wood)

In solids like wood, sound travels as both longitudinal (compression) and transverse (shear) waves. Wood has a speed of about 3300-4000 m/s depending on the type.

  • Longitudinal waves: Compression along grain
  • Transverse waves: Perpendicular to grain
  • Faster along grain than across grain

Liquids (Water)

In liquids, sound travels primarily as longitudinal waves through compression and rarefaction. Water transmits sound efficiently at about 1480 m/s.

  • Only longitudinal waves (no shear strength)
  • Faster in saltwater than freshwater
  • Temperature affects speed significantly

Gases (Air)

In gases, sound travels as longitudinal waves through compression and rarefaction of molecules. Speed depends on temperature and molecular weight.

  • Higher temperature = faster speed
  • Lower molecular weight = faster speed
  • Only longitudinal waves
