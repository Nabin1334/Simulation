What Are Sound Waves?

Sound waves are vibrations that travel through air, water, or solids. When something vibrates, it pushes air molecules together and pulls them apart, creating waves that travel to your ears!

Spring Analogy

Imagine a slinky! When you push and pull it, the coils bunch up (compression) and spread out (rarefaction). Sound waves work the same way with air molecules!

Real World Examples

Musical Instruments

Guitar strings vibrate to create sound waves that travel through the air to your ears.

Your Voice

When you talk, your vocal cords vibrate and create sound waves.

Speakers

Speaker cones move back and forth to push air and create sound.

Underwater Sounds

Dolphins use sound to communicate underwater where sound travels faster!

Key Concepts

  • Compression: Where air molecules are squeezed together (HIGH pressure)
  • Rarefaction: Where air molecules are spread apart (LOW pressure)
  • Amplitude: Wave height = LOUDNESS of sound
  • Frequency: Waves per second = PITCH of sound
  • Wavelength: Distance between compressions
