How It Works

The Molecular Theory of Magnetism states that every molecule of a magnetic substance is a complete magnet in itself, with a north and south pole. The overall magnetic property of a material depends on how these molecular magnets are arranged.

Ferromagnetic materials (like iron) have molecular magnets that align in the same direction, creating strong magnetism even without an external field.

Paramagnetic materials have randomly oriented molecular magnets that partially align when an external magnetic field is applied.

Diamagnetic materials have molecular magnets that oppose an external magnetic field, creating weak repulsion.

Antiferromagnetic materials have alternating molecular magnets that cancel each other out, resulting in no net magnetization.
