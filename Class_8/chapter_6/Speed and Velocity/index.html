Physics Concepts Explained

What is SPEED?

Speed tells you HOW FAST something is moving. It's a scalar quantity - it only has magnitude (size/number) but no direction information.

Think of a car's speedometer: it shows 60 mph but doesn't tell you which way you're going.

Speed = Distance ÷ Time
Example: 60 km/h

What is VELOCITY?

Velocity tells you HOW FAST AND IN WHICH DIRECTION something is moving. It's a vector quantity - it has both magnitude and direction.

Think of GPS navigation: it shows "60 mph heading North" - both speed and direction.

Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time
Example: 60 km/h East

Key Physics Insight

In this simulation, both cars move EAST. Since direction is the same, their speeds and velocities have the same numerical values. The difference becomes clear when direction changes!

Both cars have velocity = speed + "EAST" direction

Learning Objective

Speed tells you how fast something moves (scalar quantity: magnitude only). Velocity tells you both how fast AND which direction (vector quantity: magnitude + direction). In this simulation, both cars move EAST, so their velocities equal their speeds plus "EAST" direction. The key insight: when direction matters (like in navigation), velocity gives you complete information.

Remember: Speed (scalar, how fast) ≠ Velocity (vector, how fast + which way)
