Check website trust

Not everything online is accurate. Before using a website for school work, check who runs it and when it was last updated. The domain extension — the ending of a web address — is the quickest first clue.

Domain extension guide

.gov
Government
Official government sites — most trustworthy for factual information
.edu
Educational
Universities and schools — generally accurate and well-reviewed
.org
Organizations
Non-profits and bodies — usually reliable, but still check who runs it
.com
Commercial
Anyone can own — always verify information against a second source

The websites below are free, reliable, and safe to use for any student. They are maintained by governments, universities, or well-known educational organisations.

Recommended websites for students

  • ncert.nic.in — Official NCERT textbooks for all grades, free to read and download
  • khanacademy.org — Free video lessons, articles, and practice exercises covering every subject and grade level
  • phet.colorado.edu — Free interactive science and maths simulations made by the University of Colorado
  • moe.gov.np — Nepal Ministry of Education — official curriculum, policy, and resource portal
  • diksha.gov.in — Government digital learning platform with free content for all grade levels
  • en.wikipedia.org — Good starting point for background reading; always confirm key facts in a second source

Copyright rules for school projects

Copyright is a legal right that protects the work of its creator — text, images, music, and videos. When you find something online, that does not mean it is free to copy or use. Using someone else's work without credit is called plagiarism and can result in serious consequences at school. Always cite your sources and use freely licensed materials.

❌ Do not do this
  • Copy-paste entire webpages into your work
  • Use copyrighted images without permission or credit
  • Download paid books or courses for free illegally
  • Submit someone else's work as your own
  • Share pirated PDFs or files with others
✅ Do this instead
  • Read the material, then write the idea in your own words
  • Use images marked as Creative Commons or public domain
  • Write a reference or citation at the end of your work
  • Use free official educational platforms (listed below)
  • Ask your teacher if you are unsure whether something is allowed
