Computer Virus Explained

A comprehensive guide to understanding computer viruses, their types, history, and prevention

What is a Computer Virus?

A computer virus is a type of malicious software (malware) that attaches itself to legitimate programs or files and spreads to other computers. Much like a biological virus, it needs a host to survive and can cause damage to your system.

Important Fact: The term "virus" was first academically defined in 1983 to describe self-replicating computer programs.

When activated, a computer virus can:

  • Corrupt or delete your files
  • Slow down your computer performance
  • Steal personal information like passwords and financial data
  • Take control of your system for malicious purposes
  • Spread to other devices on your network

Security Tip: Never open email attachments from unknown senders or download software from untrusted websites. These remain the most common infection vectors.

Types of Computer Viruses

Here are the main types of computer viruses that users should be aware of:

Worms

Self-replicating malware that spreads across networks without requiring user interaction.

How it spreads: Through network vulnerabilities, email attachments, or instant messaging.

Damage caused: Consumes bandwidth, deletes files, installs backdoors.

Real example: The Morris Worm (1988) infected approximately 10% of internet-connected computers.

Prevention: Keep systems updated with security patches.

Trojans

Malware disguised as legitimate software to trick users into installation.

How it spreads: Through software downloads, email attachments, or malicious websites.

Damage caused: Creates backdoors, steals data, downloads additional malware.

Real example: Zeus Trojan (2007) targeted banking information.

Prevention: Only download software from official sources.

Ransomware

Encrypts files and demands payment (ransom) to restore access.

How it spreads: Through phishing emails, exploit kits, or remote desktop attacks.

Damage caused: Data encryption, financial extortion, business disruption.

Real example: WannaCry (2017) affected 200,000+ computers across 150 countries.

Prevention: Regular backups and network segmentation.

Spyware

Secretly monitors user activity to collect personal information.

How it spreads: Through software bundles, malicious ads, or phishing.

Damage caused: Identity theft, financial fraud, privacy invasion.

Prevention: Use privacy-focused browsers and anti-spyware tools.

Adware

Displays unwanted advertisements, often bundled with free software.

How it spreads: Through software bundles, misleading installers, or malicious ads.

Damage caused: System slowdown, privacy concerns, exposure to more malware.

Prevention: Read installation prompts carefully and use ad-blockers.

Boot Sector Virus

Infects the master boot record, making removal difficult.

How it spreads: Through infected removable media or downloads.

Damage caused: Prevents system boot, corrupts files, spreads to other media.

Real example: Stoned virus (1987) displayed "Your computer is now stoned" message.

Prevention: Write-protect boot sectors and scan removable media.

Protection & Prevention

Essential practices to protect your computer from viruses:

Use Antivirus Software

Install reputable antivirus software from trusted vendors and keep it updated with the latest definitions.

Regular Updates

Enable automatic updates for your operating system and applications to patch security vulnerabilities.

Practice Safe Browsing

Avoid suspicious links, use strong unique passwords, and enable multi-factor authentication.

Backup Your Data

Follow the 3-2-1 backup rule: 3 copies, 2 different media, 1 offsite location.

Additional Tips: Enable your firewall, use a VPN on public Wi-Fi, and regularly review account activities for suspicious behavior.

Virus History Timeline

Significant moments in the history of computer viruses:

1971 - Creeper

First experimental virus created by Bob Thomas at BBN Technologies. Displayed "I'm the creeper, catch me if you can!" on ARPANET systems.

1982 - Elk Cloner

Created by 15-year-old Rich Skrenta for Apple II systems. Spread via floppy disks and displayed a poem on the 50th boot.

1988 - Morris Worm

Created by Robert Tappan Morris, a Cornell graduate student. Infected approximately 10% of internet-connected computers.

1999 - Melissa

Macro virus that spread via email attachments. Caused an estimated $80 million in damage.

2004 - MyDoom

Fastest-spreading email worm at the time. Caused an estimated $38 billion in damage.

2017 - WannaCry

Global ransomware attack exploiting Windows SMB vulnerability. Affected 200,000+ computers across 150 countries.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I detect if my computer has a virus?

Common indicators include: Unusually slow performance, frequent crashes, unexpected pop-up ads, programs starting automatically, changed browser settings, and unfamiliar processes in Task Manager (Windows) or Activity Monitor (Mac). Run a full scan with updated antivirus software if you notice these symptoms.

Are Apple devices immune to viruses?

No platform is immune. While macOS has historically had fewer viruses than Windows (due to market share and different security architectures), Mac malware does exist and is increasing. Always use security software regardless of your operating system.

What should I do if I accidentally install malware?

Follow these steps: 1) Immediately disconnect from the internet, 2) Boot in Safe Mode, 3) Run a full antivirus scan, 4) Use malware removal tools, 5) Change all passwords from a clean device, 6) Restore from backup if available.

How effective are free antivirus programs?

Free antivirus can provide basic protection but often lacks advanced features. Reputable free antivirus software can detect most widespread malware. However, paid versions typically offer better protection against zero-day threats, ransomware, and include additional features like firewalls and password managers.
